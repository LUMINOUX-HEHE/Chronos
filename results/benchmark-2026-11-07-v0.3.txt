Interpretation:
- The adaptive policy selected sequential execution for all 15 inexpensive graph cases, avoiding the much larger forced-parallel overhead.
- Adaptive evaluation still adds a small amount of report/metrics overhead compared with the reference evaluator on cheap workloads. The ratio is affected by the machine's load and sampling variability.
- The adaptive policy selected parallel execution for the compute-heavy graph: 16 independent HashRounds operations, 150,000 rounds per operation, 32 total events, maximum width 16.
- On that workload, forced-parallel median was 1.1283 ms and adaptive median was 1.0694 ms, versus 7.805 ms sequential. Adaptive was approximately 7.30x faster than sequential at the median.
- This is a positive result for one bounded synthetic workload on one laptop, not a general performance guarantee.
- HashRounds is a deterministic CPU workload, not a cryptographic primitive.
- Follow-up: sweep thresholds/worker counts and benchmark heterogeneous costs, seeded random DAGs, other machines, and a mature external runtime.
